\section{Motivation and Objectives}

Most novice players first judge Pokemon by raw base stats. Our project asks whether that assumption still works in competitive play. We are not saying stats are meaningless. Our claim is simple: raw stats matter, but competitive success also depends on teammates, usage, moves, items, abilities, competitive roles, and synergy.

The goal is a narrative visualization for novice viewers. The experience follows a Martini Glass structure \cite{segel2010narrative}. It begins with a guided contradiction between stats and usage, then moves into role and relationship based explanations and open exploration. The force-directed team synergy network is the main visualization because competitive success depends on team relationships, not just individual stats. Supporting views are included only when they help explain competitive success.

\section{Driving Application and Datasets}

The driving application is competitive Pokemon team analysis for VGC 2022. The user-facing question is: \textit{What really makes a Pokemon strong?} The visual system helps viewers see why raw stats alone cannot explain competitive success.

We use the Complete Competitive Pokemon Database (May 2022). It includes Pokemon/form records, base stats, VGC rules, monthly usage, teammate co-usage, moves, items, and abilities \cite{carbone2022competitive}. We also use a Pokemon image dataset for visual identity \cite{singh32000pokemon}. After preprocessing, the working data contains:

\begin{itemize}
  \item 1,098 Pokemon/form rows, including 228 rows with numeric usage data.
  \item 2,606 cleaned teammate co-usage edges.
  \item 2,967 build usage rows covering moves, items, and abilities.
  \item Image references for all 1,098 Pokemon/form rows.
\end{itemize}

The preprocessing step filters teammate relationships to meaningful co-usage values and limits build data to the most common moves, items, and abilities. This creates a clearer story structure. Pokemon become nodes, teammate relationships become edges, and build data becomes evidence for competitive roles and team function.

\section{Challenges to Address}

The main design challenge is restraint. The data includes stats, teammates, usage, moves, items, abilities, and rules. It would be easy to build a broad analytics dashboard. That would weaken the project. The story needs controlled attention. The viewer should first see that raw stats are not enough, then use the network to understand team value.

The biggest remaining risks are readability and pacing. Force-directed networks can become dense. To manage this, we limit the network to a meaningful competitive subset. We use selection-based fading, add labels only when they clarify the story, and treat the detail panel as an explanation surface rather than a separate dashboard. Another risk is making claims too strong. Usage is a proxy for competitive relevance, not a perfect measure of strength. The final report and interface will state this limit clearly.

\section{Background and Related Work}

We use narrative visualization principles from Segel and Heer \cite{segel2010narrative}. The guided opening gives context. The later network interaction supports exploration. This matches the Martini Glass structure.

D3 works well for this project because it gives us direct control over scales, brushing, SVG marks, force simulation, highlighting, and transitions \cite{d3js}. A force-directed layout fits the story because competitive value is about relationships. Focus-and-context interaction helps viewers inspect one Pokemon while still seeing the larger team ecosystem.

\section{Method: Design and Implementation}

The current prototype already supports the main story flow. It starts with a stats-based assumption, reveals a contradiction in usage, then moves into a team network where relationships and roles explain more of the pattern. The views work together as one guided system before open exploration.

\pairedfigures{0.98}{figure_scatterplot_contradiction.png}{Figure 1. The scatterplot shows that high base stats weakly predict usage.}{0.98}{figure_connectivity_bridge.png}{Figure 2. The bridge shifts attention from raw stats to teammate connectivity.}

The design has four story beats. First, the introduction states the common assumption: stronger Pokemon simply have higher stats. Second, the stats-vs-usage scatterplot shows the contradiction. Zacian Crowned Sword has high stats and high usage, while Zamazenta Crowned Shield and Mewtwo show that high stats can still lead to low usage. Incineroar has moderate stats but high usage and connectivity. Third, the network explains why. Competitive value appears through teammate links, support roles, and team fit. Fourth, the viewer can explore the same idea through selected Pokemon, brushed scatterplot regions, and role evidence. The authored Incineroar reveal comes before open exploration because novice viewers may not know what to look for in a dense network. It teaches an interpretation frame before asking viewers to investigate other cases.

The force-directed network remains the main explanation layer. It externalizes team structure that is hidden in isolated stat rows: node size shows usage, node color shows primary type, and edge strength shows repeated teammate fit. Selection isolates local teammate relationships while preserving the larger network context. This focus-and-context structure lets the network explain both the selected Pokemon and the surrounding ecosystem.

\compactfigure{0.55}{figure_incineroar_network.png}{Figure 3. Selecting Incineroar reveals strong teammate connectivity and support-oriented role evidence despite only moderate base stats.}

The supporting scatterplot stays smaller. It starts the contradiction: raw stat total weakly explains usage. Brushing connects statistical outliers, such as high-stat low-usage Pokemon, to their place in the team network. The detail panel reframes moves, items, abilities, and teammates as role evidence, shifting the panel from static facts toward causal explanations of team function.

Important changes since the proposal include a stronger focus-and-context structure, linked reasoning across the scatterplot, network, and detail panel, an Incineroar-centered narrative reveal, and build evidence in the role explanation layer. We also clarified one design rule: extra charts should not overpower the network. The next refinement focuses on the detail panel. Early versions felt too much like a Pokedex fact list. We are redesigning this layer so moves, items, abilities, and teammates explain why specific Pokemon succeed competitively.

\section{Evaluation Plan}

We will evaluate the system through informal walkthroughs and design inspection. Because this is a narrative visualization, the main question is whether viewers change how they interpret competitive strength, not whether they simply operate the controls.

First, we will ask classmates or teammates to move through the guided story. We will check whether they understand why the scatterplot contradiction leads into connectivity and then into the network. This matters because the viewer should see the network as an explanation layer, not as a separate chart. We will also ask whether the authored Incineroar reveal explains support value rather than feeling like a random default selection.

Second, we will evaluate linked storytelling interactions. We will check whether brushing, selection, node size, edge strength, focus-and-context fading, and role annotations help viewers trace evidence across views instead of simply browsing names. These checks test whether interaction reinforces the reasoning chain and makes team structure interpretable. We will also test whether labels and role evidence remain readable on common laptop and presentation screens.

Our success measure is story clarity, not feature count. If viewers can explain the transition from raw stats to network role evidence, the design is working. If they treat the system like a broad Pokedex browser, or return to judging Pokemon only by stats, the design has failed.
\section{Preliminary Results}

The current prototype supports the main project claim. Across the cleaned usage-tracked dataset, total base stats and usage show a weak correlation of 0.194, while team connectivity degree and usage show a much stronger correlation of 0.903. This justifies making the network the primary explanation layer, not a decorative supporting view.

The case studies further support this pattern. Zacian Crowned Sword matches the high-stat, high-usage expectation, while Zamazenta Crowned Shield and Mewtwo have very high stats but lower usage. Incineroar shows the opposite pattern: 530 base stats, high usage, strong connectivity, and repeated support-oriented builds. Its moves, ability, and teammate relationships show why the network is central: it reveals competitive structure not visible from isolated statistics.
\section{Plan of Activities}

The project has moved from assembling coordinated views toward refining a guided narrative reasoning system. Remaining work prioritizes explanation clarity, authored transitions, and viewer interpretation flow. Any added view must strengthen the central thesis: why certain Pokemon succeed competitively. Examples include role-causality flow cards, ecosystem explanation panels, and evidence-tracing interaction layers.
