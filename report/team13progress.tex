\section{Motivation and Objectives}

Most novice players first judge Pokemon by raw base stats. Our project asks whether that assumption still works in competitive play. We are not saying stats are meaningless. Our claim is simple: raw stats matter, but competitive success also depends on teammates, usage, moves, items, abilities, competitive roles, and synergy.

The goal is a narrative visualization for novice viewers. The experience follows a Martini Glass structure \cite{segel2010narrative}. It begins with a guided contradiction between stats and usage, then moves into role- and relationship-based explanations and open exploration. The force-directed team synergy network is the main visualization because competitive success depends on team relationships, not only individual stats. Supporting views are included only when they help explain why certain Pokemon succeed competitively.

\section{Driving Application and Datasets}

The driving application is competitive Pokemon team analysis for VGC 2022. The user-facing question is: \textit{What really makes a Pokemon strong?} This framing keeps the project from becoming a generic Pokedex browser. The visual system should help viewers compare individual traits, then see why those traits are incomplete without team context.

We use the Complete Competitive Pokemon Database (May 2022). It includes Pokemon/form records, base stats, VGC rules, monthly usage, teammate co-usage, moves, items, and abilities \cite{carbone2022competitive}. We also use a Pokemon image dataset for visual identity \cite{singh32000pokemon}. After preprocessing, the working data contains:

\begin{itemize}
  \item 1,098 Pokemon/form rows, including 228 rows with numeric usage data.
  \item 2,606 cleaned teammate co-usage edges.
  \item 2,967 build usage rows covering moves, items, and abilities.
  \item Image references for all 1,098 Pokemon/form rows.
\end{itemize}

The preprocessing step converts numeric fields and flags missing base stats. It also derives offensive and defensive scores, normalizes names for image lookup, keeps VGC 2022 eligibility information, filters team edges to meaningful co-usage values, and limits build rows to the most common moves, items, and abilities. This creates a clear view structure. Pokemon are nodes, teammate pairs are edges, and build usage rows give evidence for role and team function.

\section{Challenges to Address}

The main design challenge is restraint. The data includes stats, usage, teammates, moves, items, abilities, images, and rules. It would be easy to build a broad analytics dashboard. That would weaken the project. Our story needs controlled attention. The viewer should first see that raw stats are not enough, then use the network to understand team value.

The biggest remaining risks are readability and pacing. Force-directed networks can become dense. To manage this, we limit the network to a meaningful competitive subset. We use selection-based fading, add labels only when they clarify the story, and treat the detail panel as an explanation surface rather than a separate dashboard. Another risk is overclaiming. Usage is a proxy for competitive relevance, not a perfect measure of strength. The final report and interface will state this limit clearly.

\section{Background and Related Work}

We use narrative visualization principles from Segel and Heer \cite{segel2010narrative}. The guided opening gives the viewer context. The later network interaction lets the viewer explore. This matches the Martini Glass structure.

D3 works well for this project because it gives us direct control over scales, brushing, SVG marks, force simulation, highlighting, and transitions \cite{d3js}. A force-directed layout fits the story because competitive value is about relationships. Focus-and-context interaction also helps because viewers can inspect one Pokemon while still seeing the larger team ecosystem.

\section{Method: Design and Implementation}

The current prototype already supports the main story flow. It starts with a stats-based assumption, reveals a contradiction in usage, then moves into a team network where relationships and roles explain more of the pattern. The views work together as one guided system, with open exploration after the network reveal.

\compactfigure{0.62}{figure_scatterplot_contradiction.png}{Figure 1. The scatterplot introduces the central contradiction of the project: high base stats alone weakly predict competitive usage.}

The design has four story beats. First, the introduction states the common assumption: stronger Pokemon simply have higher stats. Second, the stats-vs-usage scatterplot shows the contradiction. Zacian Crowned Sword has high stats and high usage. Zamazenta Crowned Shield and Mewtwo show that high stats can still lead to low usage. Incineroar has moderate stats but high usage and connectivity. Third, the network explains why. Competitive value appears through repeated teammate links, support roles, and team fit. Fourth, the viewer can explore the same idea through selected Pokemon, brushed scatterplot regions, and role evidence.

\compactfigure{0.62}{figure_connectivity_bridge.png}{Figure 2. The narrative bridge shifts the explanation from raw stats toward teammate connectivity and repeated team relationships.}

The force-directed network remains the main character. Node size shows usage percentage, node color shows primary type, and edge strength shows repeated teammate fit. Selection isolates a local teammate ecosystem while preserving the larger network context. This helps viewers compare one Pokemon's role against the wider competitive environment.

\compactfigure{0.58}{figure_incineroar_network.png}{Figure 3. Selecting Incineroar reveals strong teammate connectivity and support-oriented role evidence despite only moderate base stats.}

The supporting scatterplot is intentionally smaller. It starts the contradiction: raw stat total weakly explains usage. Brushing connects statistical outliers, such as high-stat low-usage Pokemon, to their place in the team network. The detail panel reframes moves, items, abilities, and teammates as evidence for competitive role value.

Important changes since the proposal include a stronger focus-and-context structure, linked reasoning across the scatterplot, network, and detail panel, an Incineroar-centered narrative reveal, and build evidence in the role explanation layer. We also clarified one design rule: extra charts should not overpower the network. The next refinement is the detail panel. Early versions felt too much like a Pokedex fact list. We are redesigning this layer so moves, items, abilities, and teammates explain why specific Pokemon succeed competitively.

\section{Evaluation Plan}

We will evaluate the system with design checks and small user feedback. First, we will run a task-based walkthrough with classmates or teammates. We will ask whether users can answer: (1) why raw stats alone are not enough, (2) why Incineroar is an important contradiction case, (3) what an edge means in the network, and (4) how brushing the scatterplot changes the network. Second, we will check whether selections stay synchronized across views. We will also check whether labels, legends, and annotations are readable on common laptop and presentation screen sizes.

Our success measure is story clarity, not feature count. A successful viewer should leave with the idea that competitive strength is relational. If users treat the app like a broad Pokedex browser, the design has failed. If they can explain how usage, teammates, builds, and network position support the claim, the design is working.

\section{Preliminary Results}

The current data supports the project claim. Across the cleaned usage-tracked data, total base stats and usage have a weak correlation of 0.194. Team connectivity degree and usage have a much stronger correlation of 0.903. This does not prove that connectivity alone causes success. It supports our story direction: competitive relevance is easier to understand when we include team relationships.

The case studies make this easier to read. Zacian Crowned Sword is powerful by both stats and usage, so it matches the beginner assumption. Zamazenta Crowned Shield and Mewtwo challenge the assumption because they have very high stats but low usage in this dataset. Incineroar is the main reveal. It has a moderate 530 base stat total, but high usage, many teammate connections, and a support build pattern centered on role value. Its common moves and ability help explain why it fits many teams.
