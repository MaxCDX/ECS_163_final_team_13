ECS 163 proposal prototype

AI risk is not evenly distributed across work.

Salary can suggest bargaining power, but it does not fully explain which jobs appear exposed to automation. This prototype starts the story by comparing pay with automation risk across industries, regions, and years.

5,000 AI-related job postings, 2010-2025

Dataset

A labor-market snapshot with uneven exposure.

The data contains job postings from 2010 through 2025 with salary, industry, location, AI intensity, automation risk, and displacement-risk labels. The goal is not to rank individual careers, but to show why AI impact is better understood as a pattern across job context instead of a single high-pay versus low-pay divide.

Interactive demo

Look for high-risk work at every salary level.

Each point is a job posting. Filter by industry and scrub through years to see how exposed roles appear across the salary range.

A horizontal spread of high-risk points would support the project thesis: higher salaries do not automatically remove exposure to AI-driven automation.

Final project direction

From demo to narrative visualization.

The final version will turn this first scatter plot into a stronger guided story: an opening claim, annotated transitions, richer industry and regional comparisons, and one advanced D3 visualization that makes uneven AI impact feel concrete.
